# Sun & Moon: plan and architecture

A live, full-screen storybook in the browser, told once (52 s) and then at rest.
A storybook stage lights up and its curtains part; a painted moon (top-left) and
sun (bottom-right), the moon hung on a string and the sun held on a rod, like
puppets, speak to each other (a recorded dialogue, subtitled near each speaker)
while their hands slowly reach until the fingertips touch. The light of that
touch opens like an iris into a close-up of their eyes, which wake, glow, blink
like a person's and blush while a recorded poem plays as kinetic typography;
then the curtains close and the page offers "Press Enter to restart".
Vite + TypeScript + three.js; no other runtime libraries.

## 0. How the direction changed

1. **ASCII version (first brief, PROMPT.md).** An ornate painting rendered as
   ASCII characters. Archived, complete and runnable, in `archive/ascii-v1.tar.gz`.
2. **Storybook version (v2).** Three new images ("Sun and Moon.jpeg", Hands.png,
   Faces2.png); sun bottom-right and moon top-left (never stacked vertically); a
   slow puppet-show reach until the hands meet, then the eyes; sun rose-pink and
   moon blue as translucent glazes that blend; human blinks; "story book
   animation, 2D cut out, motion comic, animatic"; no ASCII at all. A 45 s loop
   behind a title card.
3. **v3 (current): sound, words, a stage.** The user added two recordings and a
   reference video:
   - `audio.mp4` (a spoken dialogue: a woman, the moon; a man, the sun) plays from
     the curtains to the touch; the reference video's audio (a spoken poem) plays
     from the touch with the eyes. Both are synced to the picture.
   - Subtitles for both: storybook captions for the dialogue, and for the poem
     kinetic typography "like that video" but in the piece's own colours and type.
   - No title card: "an opening like a storybook animation", curtains that part
     to begin the tale and close at its end, taking "Krish Trish and Baltiboy" as
     a reference for the idea only, drawn "in the art style and theme of our Sun
     and Moon picture" (engraved gold, watercolour, its suns, moons, stars and
     clouds; not bluish, not a copy of the cartoon).
   - It plays once and stops; at the end "Press Enter to restart" (not "Watch again").
   - The fingertips must only ever approach (an earlier version pulled them back).

PROMPT.md is the original brief and is left untouched; where it conflicts with the
user's later direction, the later direction wins.

## 3. Preparing the images (`npm run assets`)

`scripts/prepare_assets.py` (uv, Python 3.11, numpy / scipy / pillow / OpenCV):

1. **Gold mask.** In "Sun and Moon.jpeg" everything drawn is warm gold on a dark
   blue sky: luminance > 0.28 and red > blue.
2. **Clouds** are the gold masses connected to the bottom edge; their solid
   silhouettes become the two cloud cut-outs (also heaped over the stage's corners).
3. **Sun** = gold within 350 px of its centre, minus the clouds. **Moon** = the
   largest gold shape in its box. **Big stars** = compact gold shapes above y 470.
4. **The sky plate.** The sun, moon and stars are painted out with OpenCV's
   exemplar-based shift-map inpainting (the moon's hole is grown by 18 px so its
   dark rim goes too); inside the hole, tones come from a broad blur of a star-free
   base with fine detail kept, so no copied block shows a straight seam.
5. **Twinkle mask**: the small bright specks left in the plate.
6. **Hands**: the connected bright region around a seed point; anchors are the
   fingertip and the "shoulder".
7. **Eyelids**: each lid is traced by hand through 27-41 points from tip to tip
   where the drawn strokes meet, nudged 1 px into the stroke; `faces.ts` reads them
   per column from a small float texture.
8. `src/generated/story.ts` records sizes, boxes, anchors and lids.

## 4. Speech data (`npm run transcribe`)

`scripts/transcribe.py` runs Whisper (small.en, CPU) over both clips and writes
`src/generated/speech.ts`: every word with its start and end in seconds from its
clip's start, and a loudness envelope at 20 Hz (0..1). The captions refer to words
by index; the voices' loudness lets the speaker's light swell softly as they speak
and the eyes' glow breathe with the poem, still as a pure function of time.

## 5. The story (all in `config.ts` -> TIMELINE)

| Beat | Seconds | What happens | Sound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Theatre | 0 - 1.4 | black; the storybook stage lights up, curtains closed | silent |
| Curtains part | 1.6 - 6.2 | the drapes gather to the sides and the stage flies out; the sky behind | dialogue from **2.0** |
| Puppets enter | 1.6 - 8.0 | the moon lowered into the top-left as she speaks, the sun raised from behind the clouds as he answers | moon (woman), sun (man) |
| Reach | 7.0 - 21.4 | the hands slide out from behind their bodies and reach, only ever closer | dialogue subtitles near each speaker |
| Touch | **21.4** | the fingertips meet; a light gathers, the world hushes, the light flares | poem from **21.4** (music first) |
| Iris | 21.8 - 23.7 | the light opens like an iris into the eyes | first poem word ~23.7 |
| Eyes | 22.0 - 49.8 | the eyes wake, glow, blink like a person's, blush; the poem as kinetic type | "A BRAVE THING." ~45-46 |
| Curtains close | 47.4 - 51.6 | the drapes close over the eyes and the last words | |
| End | 52 | it stops on the closed, lit curtains; "Press Enter to restart" (touch: "Tap to restart") | |

**To retime**, edit the windows in `TIMELINE` (each is `[start, end]` seconds; keep
start < end); the audio start times are `TIMELINE.audio`. Everything else follows
(the poem's last fade follows `curtainsClose`). `npm run check:timeline` then
confirms the story still holds together.

## 6. How a frame is made

`main.ts` reads the clock, calls `storyAt(t, view)` (pure), samples the pointer,
updates the soundtrack and hands state and input to `Stage.render`:

1. **Storybook layers** (`render/stage.ts`), back to front: painted sky (its specks
   twinkle), the four hanging stars (kept inside the screen's sides), the hands,
   the moon and the sun, the clouds in front, the touch light. Each layer is a
   textured quad placed by its anchor (world units: origin at the screen centre,
   y up, the short side = 1) through a slow camera push, with a few pixels of
   pointer parallax by depth. Positions are fractions of the screen, blended by
   aspect, so the moon is always top-left and the sun bottom-right.
2. **Light.** A per-pixel lighting term: a soft coloured light from each body falls
   across every layer, swelling gently with that speaker's voice. A hush darkens
   the world slowly around the touch.
3. **Translucent glazes.** A layer keeps its own brightness and engraving and only
   takes on the hue (rose for the sun and its hand, blue for the moon and hers).
4. **Hands** are scaled so both arms span from inside their bodies to the meeting
   point, turned to point at it, and slid along that line so the fingertips land
   exactly on it at the touch. They start hidden behind their bodies and slide out
   as opaque cut-outs (a mask hides only what would show behind the body; the moon
   hand's thumb shows in one piece with its root).
5. **The eyes** (`render/faces.ts`): one full-screen quad, the picture fitted to the
   screen (on tall screens its inked span fills the width), each eyelid warped
   (section 7), glazed rose on top and blue below, irises glowing in the other
   colour and breathing with the poem's voice, star catchlights, the drawn stars
   twinkling, rose cheeks; revealed by an iris of light centred on the touch.
6. **Words** (`render/captions.ts`): a 2D canvas the size of the drawing buffer,
   cleared and drawn each frame by `dialogue.ts` or `poem.ts`, uploaded as a texture
   above the eyes (section 8).
7. **The theatre** (`render/curtains.ts`), above everything while the curtains show
   (section 10).
8. Everything renders into one half-float target, then **UnrealBloomPass** (only
   the brightest highlights), then the grade (vignette, light film grain, exposure,
   master fade) and sRGB to the screen.

At boot, `Stage.create` renders the whole scene once with every layer visible, so
every shader is compiled and every texture uploaded before the story starts (not at
the climax).

## 7. Human blinks (`blink.ts`, `render/faces.ts`)

**When.** Each face has its own schedule, generated once from a seed: uneven gaps
(2 - 5 s), sometimes a double or half blink, the second eye trailing by a few
milliseconds; none lands on the poem's biggest words. The eyes wake slowly as the
light opens and close as the curtains do.

**How fast.** The lid closes fast and accelerating (~0.1 s), rests (~0.04 s), and
opens slower, decelerating (~0.2 s). `closure(t)` in 0..1 is a pure function of time.

**How it looks.** The upper lid band slides down to the lower lid along the traced
lid lines: for a pixel between the lid's resting line and its current line, the
shader shows what lies above it, shifted down, so the lid and its lashes travel and
the skin above stretches. The moving edge is anti-aliased over one screen pixel
(width from the zoom and the edge's slope); half-closed lids keep their arch.

## 8. Words on screen (`captions.ts`, `dialogue.ts`, `poem.ts`)

Both are drawn from `state.t` and `SPEECH`, so a word appears exactly when it is
spoken, at any seek or scrub.

- **Dialogue** (2.0 - 21.4 s): storybook captions near each speaker in empty sky
  (the moon's words in the upper area, the sun's near him; never over the faces,
  hands or touch), blue for the moon and rose for the sun, IM Fell English with a
  few key words in a script or display face; each word fades and settles in as
  spoken, a soft dark halo for legibility, the line fading once said.
- **Poem** (21.4 - 51.6 s): a collage per phrase in the reference's spirit: huge
  condensed Instrument Serif, Playfair Display 900, Pinyon Script, Special Elite
  typewriter lines, IM Fell connecting words; rose and periwinkle with soft glow
  (cached sprites, no per-frame blur); accents tied to words, drawn like the
  drawing: the sun emblem's spiral during the music, an 8-point starburst on
  "hoping", a constellation on "bring me back to you", little stars, a sketched
  heart with a thread, concentric rings like a moon halo, and "A BRAVE THING." typed
  and held while the curtains close. On tall screens the lines spread into the
  space above and below the picture.

The canvas is uploaded as plain RGBA8 (an sRGB format made the driver convert every
pixel, ~30 ms a frame) and decoded in the shader; it is resized with the buffer.

## 9. Sound (`soundtrack.ts`)

Browsers keep a page silent until the visitor clicks, taps or presses a key. The
story starts by itself, silently, with the cue "Click anywhere for sound"; the first
click, tap or key brings the sound in, in step with the picture. The two clips play
through Web Audio (its own real-time thread), with `SOUND.latency` (0.1 s) of
buffering: the browsers' 'playback' preset gives only ~21 ms on Linux, and a heavy
frame then starved the sound (ticks, crackle). The clips are scheduled once and are
never restarted for small drift (that jumped mid-word); instead the audio is the
master clock: the picture leans toward the heard audio (from the device's output
timestamp) by at most `SOUND.follow.rate` (3%, invisible). Only a real jump past
`SOUND.sync.hard` (pause, scrub, restart, a hidden tab) reschedules the clips.
Only one open copy of the story plays sound (a BroadcastChannel): the one clicked last.
Each clip is tried as AAC first, then as MP3 (`SOUND.files`): VS Code's built-in
browsers cannot decode AAC (nor Opus), yet answer canPlayType with "probably", so
the fallback is on a failed decode.

## 10. The theatre (`render/curtains.ts`)

Drawn like the Sun and Moon painting: an old engraving in gold ink over night
tones. Drapes of deep wine cloth, engraved in fine sepia lines along their folds
(cross-hatched only in the deepest valleys), washed like watercolour, gilt-stamped
with the painting's suns (wavy rays), crescents and faceted stars; a gilt
proscenium around panels of night sky with the moon and sun in its corners; a
swagged valance with sun and moon medallions; "Sol and Luna" in a gilt cartouche; the
painting's own clouds heaped over the front corners; a night-ink stage floor.
What is easier to paint than to compute (print, borders, band, corners, valance,
title, watercolour wash) is drawn once at startup on 2D canvases with seeded
randomness; one fragment shader folds and engraves the cloth and composes the
stage. `state.curtain.light` brings the stage up from black; `state.curtain.open`
parts the drapes (gathering their folds and print), then the stage flies out with a
small push so the storybook fills the screen; at the end the reverse, and the cloth
under the "Press Enter to restart" words is kept free of print.

## 11. Checks

- `npm run check:timeline`: determinism, exact time (`?t=X` shows exactly X), no
  jumps at 240 Hz, the moon top-left and the sun bottom-right on six screen shapes
  (16:9, 4:3, 21:9, square, phone, tablet), the fingertips only ever approaching and
  exactly touching at the touch, human blink timings, the theatre black at the
  start with the curtains closed at both ends; with and without
  prefers-reduced-motion.
- `npm run verify` (dev server running): the beats in landscape and on a phone,
  30 fps strips across a blink of each face, and the words' canvas holding words
  during the dialogue (12 s) and the poem (30.9 s). It exits non-zero when a page
  throws, fails to boot or logs an error (GL errors included), or a check fails.
- `node scripts/perf.mjs`: fps on the real GPU.

## 12. Interaction, UI, robustness

The story autoplays on load (silent until the first click/tap/key, see section 9),
plays once and rests on the closed curtains; "Press Enter to restart" (touch: "Tap
to restart") then appears, centred, its bottom at 18% of the screen height. Keys:
Space pause, arrows scrub 2 s, R restart, F fullscreen, M mute, H hide all UI,
Enter restart at the end; a small hint fades after a few seconds. Pointer (or
touch-drag) gives gentle parallax. Pauses when the tab is hidden (sound too) and
resumes in step; while paused, frames are redrawn only when something they depend
on changes. Handles resizes, rotation, moving to a HiDPI screen and a lost WebGL
context; prefers-reduced-motion stops the swaying, the pointer parallax and the
words' motion.
devicePixelRatio is capped at 2; if the frame rate stays low the governor lowers
the resolution in steps (fractions of the device pixel ratio, never below 0.6) and
then turns bloom off, undoing any step that does not help (a 30 Hz display keeps
full quality). URL: `?t=SECONDS&paused=1` freezes an exact moment (no sound there);
`&quality=N` locks a quality level.

`npm run build` writes `dist/` with a relative base, so it can be hosted from any
sub-folder; `npm run preview` serves it.
